Potting recipes

Two mixes, one for each group. Mix the grit through the soil, never as a layer, and leave the bottom of the pot alone.

recipe A

The dry group

Rosemary, thyme, oregano, marjoram, sage · terracotta with open drainage holes, on a saucer

  1. Start with a bare pot and an open hole.Terracotta with its drainage holes clear and a saucer underneath. No crocks, gravel or LECA at the bottom.
  2. Mix the grit through, about one third.Succulent or cactus mix with roughly ⅓ perlite (or pumice), stirred together in a bucket before it goes in the pot.
  3. Plant at nursery depth.The top of the root ball sits at the same level it did in the nursery pot. Firm gently.
  4. Optional: 2 cm of gravel on top.A thin top-dressing keeps the crown dry and stops soil splashing onto the leaves.
  5. Water deeply, then let it dry.Water until it runs out of the hole, empty the saucer, and leave it until the top 3–4 cm are dry.
recipe B

The thirsty group

Basil, Thai basil, mint, chillies · self-watering pots

  1. Mix rich and moist-holding.About 60% potting soil, 25% compost and 15% perlite, stirred together. Never garden soil.
  2. Pack the wick column firmly.Press mix hard into the wick chamber or around the cotton wick so it touches the reservoir floor without air gaps. Loose fill breaks the capillary pull.
  3. Water from the top for the first two weeks.Until the roots reach down to the wick, the reservoir cannot reach them.
  4. After that, only through the fill tube.Fill until the gauge or overflow says full and leave the surface alone.
  5. Optional: 3–5 cm of mulch.It slows evaporation in the dry sunroom air.
  6. No overflow hole? Use a tray.Neither Grow Guru trough listing mentions one, so stand it on a tray.
two myths,
busted

No gravel, crocks or LECA layer at the bottom. A coarse layer under fine soil does not drain the pot; it raises the perched water table up into the root zone. (Linda Chalker-Scott, Washington State University; summary at Horticulture.)

No water-storing crystals. Hydrogels did not reduce water use in a University of California ANR trial on container plants. (UC ANR.)

no. 4

Watering, by group

Golden rules first, then the habits that make them stick. The intervals are starting points: the calibration log below turns them into your own numbers.

Dry group

rosemary · thyme · oregano · marjoram · sage

about every 2 weeks

  1. Water about every 2 weeks once established, weekly in the first month.
  2. Finger test: dry 3–4 cm down means water. Damp means wait.
  3. Water deeply until it drains, then empty the saucer.
  4. They barely need feeding.

Thirsty group

basil · Thai basil · mint

refill every 7–10 days

  1. Refill the reservoir about every 7–10 days.
  2. Never let it sit empty for long: a dried-out wick loses its capillary pull.
  3. Flush from the top once a month to wash out salts.
  4. Liquid feed at half strength every 2–3 weeks in summer.

Chillies

Carolina Reaper · Trinidad Scorpion · Habanero

check the reservoir weekly

  1. Check the reservoir weekly, from planting onwards.
  2. In January–February at full size they may need a refill every 5–7 days: a grown superhot can drink 1–2 L a day.
  3. Same flush and feed routine as the thirsty group.
pro tips
  • Basils drooping at 3 pm?Move them out of the hot afternoon sun. A midday wilt in a full reservoir is heat, not thirst.
  • Keep the windows open.Airflow is what keeps a warm sunroom from turning into a mildew room.
  • Calibrate each reservoir.Fill it, note the date, and write down how many days it lasts. Then water by your numbers, not the calendar.
